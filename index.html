KTH InspireLab · Home-based hormone diagnostics

Women's health doesn't need more empathy. It needs more engineering.

We are building an affordable optical system that measures six hormones in urine, at home, every day. Urine instead of blood. Daily instead of a few times a year.

The problem

Hormones change every day. We measure them a few times a year.

Hormones shape metabolism, fertility, mood, sleep and cognition. Yet quantitative measurements still come from occasional blood tests in a clinic: a single snapshot of a system that never stands still.

The project

A hormone lab on a kitchen counter.

A non-invasive, reusable, home-based platform that turns an ordinary smartphone into a quantitative, multi-hormone analyser, at a price that does not exclude anyone.

  1. Sample

    Urine, at home, in a reusable container. No needles, no consumable lock-in.

  2. Illuminate

    A miniature light source and the phone's own flash light up the sample.

  3. Detect

    A KTH-made spectrometer together with the smartphone camera reads the spectrum: pixels become data.

  4. Analyse

    AI compares each reading with the user's own baseline.

For each woman

Her own hormone map

Real concentrations, tracked daily, next to mood, sleep and pain. Earlier signals for PCOS, endometriosis, primary ovarian insufficiency and the transition into menopause.

For research

Data that doesn't exist yet

Opt-in, anonymised uploads can build the first large longitudinal dataset of how hormones interact with health and well-being.

For equality

Affordable, everywhere

Designed to be low-cost and easy to use, including in settings with limited access to specialist care.

Research

Three questions, asked together.

What do we need to measure?

The optical fingerprints of key hormones and their metabolites in urine, and the conditions that make them clearest.

Protein science · hormone biology · bioanalytics

How can we measure it?

A compact, high-resolution spectrometer built from fibre gratings and a phone camera, with a sample container designed for it.

Photonics · fibre optics · spectroscopy · materials

What can the data tell us?

Signal correction, validation against laboratory assays, and patterns that sparse measurements cannot show.

Signal analysis · AI · longitudinal data

How we work

Different disciplines, one problem.

No single discipline can solve this alone. Photonics, hormone biology and data science meet in every decision we make.

Inclusion and shared leadership are our starting point, not our goal

We don't treat inclusion and joint leadership as targets to reach later. They are where the project begins: not a policy added on top of the science, but how a project about gender equality has to be run.

If women's health needs more engineering, engineering needs more than one name at the top.

What that means in practice

  • Three PIs, one projectScientific decisions are taken jointly, every week.
  • Shared supervisionStudents are co-supervised across disciplines.
  • Transparent creditAuthorship and attribution are distributed openly.
  • Room for every voice"I don't know, can you explain?" is a normal part of how we work.
  • Deliberate distribution of opportunitiesTalks, visibility and responsibility are shared on purpose.
  • Women's data, governed by womenData sharing is opt-in, anonymised and secure.
