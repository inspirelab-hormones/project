Do your master's thesis on the future of women's health

For master's students in nanotechnology, applied physics or biomedicine who want their thesis to count, scientifically and for gender equality.

The project

Hormones change every day, but we measure them only a few times a year, in a clinic, from blood. The Hormones Project, funded by KTH InspireLab, is building an affordable, home-based optical system that measures six hormones in urine, every day. The aim is earlier diagnosis of conditions such as endometriosis and PCOS, and data that can help close the gender gap in biomedical research. Read more about the project →

Where you can contribute

We are looking for highly motivated candidates with an interest in gender equality and technology to do their master's thesis, bachelor project or project course within our InspireLab project. Projects can be experimental or AI-based, at SciLifeLab or at AlbaNova.

Biomedicine

Hormones and their metabolites in urine, sample preparation, bioanalytical validation. Mainly at SciLifeLab.

Applied physics

From detection techniques and spectroscopy development to microfabrication of the container. Mainly at AlbaNova.

In between

Multidisciplinary projects that connect the two, including AI-based signal analysis and data interpretation.

Topics are shaped together with you, based on your background, your interests and where the project is at the time you start.

Requirement

Awareness of gender equality (jämställdhet) issues, and an understanding of what this project is trying to solve in that respect: that women's health has been under-measured, under-researched and too often dismissed, and that better technology is part of the answer.

Beyond that, we look for curiosity and a willingness to learn from people trained in other disciplines. Inclusion and shared leadership are our starting point, not our goal, and you will be part of a team where questions are welcome.
