PhD position · KTH, Stockholm

Hormone sensing between biophysics and biotechnology

Help build a home-based hormone lab: light, fibre optics and a smartphone, measuring six hormones in urine, every day.

Why this project

Hormones shape metabolism, fertility, mood and sleep, yet we measure them only a few times a year, in a clinic, from blood. What happens between those measurements is largely unknown. Most diagnostic technology has also been developed with male physiology as the default, which is one reason conditions such as endometriosis and PCOS often take years to diagnose.

The Hormones Project, funded by KTH InspireLab, is building an affordable, reusable optical system that measures hormones in urine at home. A compact light source and a phone's flash illuminate a urine container, and a KTH-made detection system together with a smartphone and AI turns it into hormone levels. The targets are LH, FSH, estrogen, progesterone, cortisol and serotonin, or their metabolites.

Daily, quantitative data can lead to earlier diagnosis and help close the gender gap in biomedical research. We are looking for a PhD student to contribute to building the measurement at the heart of the project.

What you will do

You will work where biophysics meets biotechnology. Depending on your background, your work will lean towards the physical measurement or towards the biomedicine side. It will include:

  • Preparing and characterising urine samples, and validating results against established laboratory assays
  • Designing and testing the detection system

You will work closely with all three research groups, at AlbaNova and SciLifeLab, publish your results, present them at international conferences and take part in the project's outreach.

Supervision and environment

The project is jointly led by three PIs, and all three will supervise you:

  • Carlota Canalias, Professor, Department of Applied Physics: photonics, materials and integration
  • Michael Fokine, Professor, Department of Applied Physics: fibre optics and optical engineering
  • Cecilia Williams, Professor, Department of Protein Science and SciLifeLab: hormone biology and bioanalytics

Depending on your background, you will be admitted to the doctoral programme in Applied Physics–Biophysics or in Biotechnology. Whichever programme you join, we make sure you gain the complementary skills the project needs. Clinical input comes from an obstetrician–gynaecologist at Karolinska Institutet.

For us, inclusion and shared leadership are our starting point, not our goal. Decisions, supervision, credit and opportunities are shared openly, and you can expect a safe environment with close, regular follow-up from all three supervisors.

Who we are looking for

A curious, highly motivated person who wants to do a PhD across disciplines and enjoys working with people whose training differs from their own.

Requirements

  • A master's degree in biophysics, biotechnology, biomedicine, physics, or a closely related field, or at least 240 higher-education credits, of which at least 60 at advanced level
  • A genuine interest in learning new disciplines
  • Knowledge of gender equality (jämställdhet) from an intersectional perspective, and an understanding of why gender aspects matter when developing science and technology
  • Good written and spoken English

Merits

  • Laboratory experience with optical, spectroscopic, biophysical or bioanalytical methods
  • Experience of working in multidisciplinary teams

In a project that crosses disciplines, no one knows everything. You should be comfortable saying "I don't know, can you explain more?"

What we offer

  • A fully funded four-year doctoral position at KTH, with salary according to KTH's agreement for doctoral students
  • Supervision from three PIs in three disciplines
  • A project that links fundamental photonics with a real gap in women's health
  • An international, collaborative research environment in Stockholm

The position may include up to 20% departmental duties, such as teaching, which extends the employment to at most five years.
